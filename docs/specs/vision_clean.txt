3. Executive Summary
Hurrykit is a brand of fast, free, single-purpose file and media tools that run entirely in the browser, built for people who have an urgent small job and no patience for signups, watermarks or uploads. It starts with converters (image, audio, video) and grows, one department at a time, into the toolkit people reach for when they are in a hurry.
The idea in one paragraph. Millions of people each day need to turn an iPhone photo into a JPG, shrink a video for WhatsApp, or resize a photo to an exact size for an exam form. They search for the exact job, land on a tool page, and want to be done in under thirty seconds. The strongest existing sites for this (iLoveIMG, CloudConvert, FreeConvert and others) upload your file to a server, cap what you can do for free, and fund themselves with ads and paid tiers. Hurrykit follows the same one-page-per-job structure but does the work on the user's own device. That makes it private by design, removes server limits, and keeps running costs close to zero.

4. Brand Identity
Hurrykit is the name of a feeling as much as a product: the moment before a deadline, and the relief of getting the job done. "Hurry" names the urgency the user arrives with, and "kit" says there is a toolbox behind it that can grow beyond any single tool.
Why the name works
It says why people come. Nobody searches for a converter for fun. They are in a hurry, and the name meets them there.
It is easy to say and spell, in India and abroad: two common English words, pronounced as written, with no unusual letter combinations.
It does not box us in. "Kit" fits converters, resizers, calculators and AI tools alike, so no rename is needed as departments are added.
It avoids the "iLove" pattern, so it is not mistaken for, or legally close to, the brand that inspired the structure.
Honest weaknesses. "Hurry" can suggest stress. The brand answers this by making the outcome calm: the user arrived stressed and leaves relieved. The name is also two ordinary words, so it is not unique in the way an invented word would be, and brand searches will share results with unrelated things until the site earns its own ranking. Because early traffic comes from tool searches such as "heic to jpg" and not from the brand name, this matters less at the start.
Brand feeling. Calm relief, not panic. Quick, friendly, honest and reliable. Never loud, spammy, gimmicky or condescending.
Voice. Short, direct sentences. The product speaks like a helpful friend who is also in a hurry.

5. Vision and Mission
Mission. Make every small file job a thirty-second job: free, private and done on your own device.
Vision. Hurrykit becomes the first place people go, by name, when they are in a hurry with a file, a photo, a video or a form. It earns that position by being faster, more trustworthy and more complete than the alternatives, not by being louder.
What the long term looks like (all Vision tier unless marked)
A deep catalogue. Hundreds of single-purpose tools across departments (converters, media tools, India form tools, text and Hindi tools, PDF, creator tools, data tools, calculators), all on one engine and one template.
A trusted brand. Known for three promises: no signup, no watermark, and your files never leave your device. These are published, checkable and never quietly dropped.
A paid AI tier. Tools that genuinely need servers or GPUs (photo to video, enhancement, auto subtitles) sit behind paid credits, clearly separated from the free, private core.
Local depth, global reach. Strongest in India (exam forms, WhatsApp media, Hindi and Hinglish), but designed from the first line of code to work worldwide and in more languages.
Available everywhere. A fast website first, then an installable web app that works offline, and possibly a native app if usage justifies it.
What Hurrykit will never be
A downloader for content from platforms such as YouTube or Instagram, or a tool for removing DRM or bypassing paywalls.
A site that makes the user sign up, watermarks the result, or hides the real limits.
A site funded by misleading ads, fake download buttons or anything that puts users' devices at risk.
A place that collects or sells people's files or personal data.
Why these limits are part of the vision. The weakest sites in this category are the ones people distrust. A brand that keeps a few clear promises, year after year, is the one worth remembering and typing in directly.
6. Problem Statement
People with a small, urgent file job are poorly served: the tools that rank for their search make them wait, sign up, hit limits, watch ads and trust their private files to an unknown server.
The job. Someone has one file and one outcome in mind: turn an iPhone photo into a JPG, make a video small enough for WhatsApp, convert a voice note to MP3, or resize a photo to an exact size for an exam form. They usually have a deadline, often on a phone, and they do not want to learn anything. They search for the exact task, open the first result and expect it to just work.
Where the current experience fails
Too many steps. Upload, wait, queue, wait again, download. Some tools also ask for an account.
Hidden or tight limits. Free tiers cap daily conversions, file size or processing time, and the user often learns this only after uploading.
Privacy and trust. Most converters send your file to their servers. For ID photos, signatures, family videos or work documents, that is a real concern, and users cannot verify what happens to the file afterwards.
Ad-heavy, sometimes risky pages. Many sites hide the real button among ads, pop-ups and fake download buttons.
Poor mobile experience. Many tools were designed for desktop. Pages are heavy and awkward on a phone, which is where much of the demand sits.
Format friction in everyday life. iPhone photos in HEIC, WhatsApp voice notes in OPUS, and MOV or MKV videos do not open everywhere, so format problems come up again and again.
India-specific gaps. Exam and government forms demand photos and signatures at exact file sizes and dimensions, with strict rules. The sites that serve this are mostly cluttered and unreliable, and few tools speak Hindi or Hinglish.
Slow, server-bound processing. When the work happens on a shared server, speed depends on queues and the user's upload bandwidth, which on Indian mobile connections can be slow and expensive.
Who bears the cost. The student who misses a form deadline because the photo was rejected. The small business owner who cannot send a video. The professional who needs a file in a different format five minutes before a meeting. None of them is making a big decision. They just need a small job done well, and they pay in time and trust.
Why this is not going away. New formats keep arriving, devices keep disagreeing, and forms keep demanding precise specifications. The need is small, constant and always renewed.
7. Our Solution
Hurrykit solves the problem by giving every common file job its own fast page, and by doing the work on the user's device instead of a server.
Five mechanisms
One job per page. Each page targets a single search, such as "heic to jpg" or "compress image to 50kb", and does exactly that.
In-browser processing. Images use the browser's own graphics and WebAssembly codecs. Audio and video use FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly. The file never leaves the device, so there is no upload wait, no daily server quota and no privacy question to answer.
One shared system behind many pages. A tool registry, one page template and a small number of engines mean a new tool is mostly a configuration entry and a short content page. This is what makes a large catalogue possible for a small team.
Honest, visible limits. Where a file is too large or too slow for a phone, Hurrykit says so before the user waits, and lets them try anyway.
No friction by default. No signup, no watermark, no popups that stand in the way of the real button.
The thirty-second journey
The user searches "mov to mp4" and lands on the page.
They drop, paste or pick the file. Nothing is sent anywhere.
They see the file, its size and a sensible default output. Options are available but never required.
They press Convert and watch real progress.
They download the result, or on a phone save it straight to their files or share it.
Where a server is used. In Phase 1, nowhere: the site is static pages plus code that runs in the browser. In the far future (Vision tier), paid AI tools may use server or GPU processing. Those will be separated, labelled and opt-in, so the free tools keep their privacy promise.
What Hurrykit deliberately does not try to be. A full editor, a cloud storage service or an account-based platform. It is a set of quick tools, and each should feel like a light switch.
8. Product Philosophy
Hurrykit is built on ten principles, and any feature or tool that breaks one of them needs a very good reason.
One job, one page. A page does one thing, names it clearly and does it without detours.
Thirty seconds. From landing to downloaded result, the common case takes about half a minute.
Files stay on the device. Free tools do their work locally, and the claim is checkable in the browser's network panel.
No signup, no watermark. Never traded away for revenue.
Honest limits. The tool says what it cannot do before the user wastes time, and never pretends.
Quantity through a system, quality through reliability. Many tools are possible only because they share one template and engine. Each tool is allowed to ship only if it works reliably.
Defaults that just work. The best option is preselected, and advanced controls exist but never block the main path.
Calm, not shouting. The interface relieves stress instead of adding to it.
Ship small, measure, then expand. Phases open only when the previous one earns it.
Do the right thing, even when it costs. No platform downloaders, no DRM or paywall bypass, no misleading ads.
Built-in standards. These are not separate workstreams. They apply from the first commit, and every page must meet them.
Standard
What it means in practice
Responsive everywhere
Every page works on every screen size from small phones to wide desktops. There is no separate "mobile site", and a feature is not finished until it works on a phone.
Mobile-first

Fast on modest hardware
A strict page-weight and loading budget. Heavy engines load only on the pages that need them, and only when needed.

How the principles settle arguments. When two ideas conflict, the earlier principle in this list usually wins over the later one, except principles 3, 4 and 10, which are fixed.

What they share. They are time-pressed, they arrive from a search for the exact task, they will not read instructions and they will leave a site the moment it asks for an account. They do not come back because of loyalty. They come back because the tool was fast and trustworthy last time.
What differs.
Device and data. Many Indian users are on mid-range or low-end Android phones with limited memory and mobile data. This shapes file-size limits, engine download sizes and the loading strategy.
Language. Many search in English, Hindi or a mix of both. Early pages are English. Hindi pages are a Roadmap item.
Trust. Students handling ID photos and signatures are especially sensitive to where their files go. The "nothing uploaded" promise matters most to them.
How India-first and global fit together. The product, design and code are global from day one. India shapes which tools come first and which phrases pages are written for, because that is where competition is thinner and where the founder can learn fastest. Global traffic for the same conversions is expected to arrive on the same pages without extra work.
Not the audience. Enterprises that need audit trails and contracts, and anyone looking to download from platforms or remove protections. Hurrykit is not built for them and will not be extended to serve them.

Unlike converter sites that upload your file and cap your free use, Hurrykit works locally, so it is private, has no server queue and has no daily quota.

How positioning changes over time. Early on, "private and free" is the differentiator. Once the catalogue is large, "the whole toolkit in one place" becomes the stronger claim. When AI tools arrive, "the free, private core" remains the promise, and AI is clearly labelled as the separate, paid part.

D. Indirect competition
The operating system. Windows 11 does not open HEIC photos by default. A 2021 article says it needs the HEIF Image Extensions and, for HEVC video, the paid HEVC Video Extensions ($0.99 then). This is why HEIC conversion searches exist, but it may change, so check current Windows behaviour. iPhones and Macs can export to JPG themselves.
Desktop apps. VLC, HandBrake and the FFmpeg command line. Free, powerful and trusted by technical users, but they require installation and know-how.
AI search and chat answers. Search engines and AI assistants increasingly answer "how do I convert X" directly, sometimes without a click. This is a threat to traffic, not to the tools themselves.
What the incumbents are good at
Search presence. Years of ranking, backlinks and brand recall for the head terms. This is their strongest asset and cannot be matched quickly.
Breadth. Zamzar-style format coverage and document conversions that need server software.
Heavy jobs. Large video, OCR and AI work are easier on a powerful server than in a phone's browser.
Revenue machines. They have paying users, so they can invest in speed, support and marketing.
Where they are structurally weak
Server economics force limits. Every conversion costs them compute and bandwidth, so free use must be capped (CloudConvert's 10 conversions a day and FreeConvert's 20 minutes a day are examples). Hurrykit's marginal cost per conversion is near zero because the user's device does the work.
Upload and wait. Speed depends on the user's upload bandwidth and the provider's queue.
Privacy cannot be proved. A user must trust a server's deletion policy.
Ads and upsell pressure. Free tiers exist to sell paid ones, which shapes the whole experience.
Where Hurrykit is weak
No search authority at launch. A new domain starts with no backlinks. Months pass before head terms are realistic.
Browser limits. Large videos can fail or crawl on a low-end phone, and iOS Safari is stricter than desktop Chrome.
Narrower formats. Hurrykit will cover common formats, not hundreds.
No technology moat. Others can build the same thing. VERT already proves it.
Brand. An unknown name competes against names people already trust.
Strategy that follows
Do not fight on the head terms first ("mp4 converter", "jpg to png"). Win the long tail: specific pairs such as "heic to jpg", "opus to mp3" and "mov to mp4", then climb.
Own a niche early. India exam photo and signature tools are fragmented and quality is uneven. Doing it accurately and reliably is a realistic first foothold.
Make the private claim visible and provable. It is the single difference that is hard for server-based competitors to match.
Beat them on mobile. Fast load, big touch targets, no popups, save-to-phone that works.
Ship breadth through the shared system, because a bare grid of tools is not enough, but a large, reliable catalogue is.

12. Business Model
Hurrykit's free tools stay free; money comes from scale, through ads first, then an optional paid tier, and later paid AI credits. Because processing happens on the user's device, running costs stay close to zero, so almost every rupee of revenue is margin. The real cost is the founder's time.

15. SEO Strategy
Search is the main way people will find Hurrykit. The plan is to earn rankings page by page, with real content on every page, in waves small enough that quality never slips. No ranking is guaranteed, and the plan assumes slow early results on a new site.
Step 1: Set up the foundations (before any page goes live)
Register the site in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and verify ownership.
Use one preferred domain and force HTTPS. Redirect any alternate host permanently.
Generate robots.txt and an XML sitemap from the tool registry, split by page type, and submit the sitemap.
Give every page a unique title, meta description, one H1 and a self-referencing canonical URL.
Make pages fast and stable: fast first paint, no layout jumps, conversion engines loaded only when a tool is used.
Set up privacy-friendly analytics and a spreadsheet to track rankings and clicks per page.
Step 2: Research the keywords
Build the seed list from the tool registry (every format pair and job).
Collect search volume and competition from free sources: Google Search Console once data exists, Google autocomplete and "people also ask", Google Trends for India versus global, and a free keyword tool.
Look at the top results for each target phrase and note what the page needs that competitors lack.
Group phrases by intent: do-it-now ("heic to jpg"), how-to ("open heic on windows"), and compare ("heic vs jpg").
Score each phrase by demand, competition and fit with Hurrykit, and write the result into the registry as the page's primary phrase.
Treat volumes as estimates, not promises.
Step 3: Pick the first wave
Start with the highest-demand, easiest-to-win conversions where a browser tool is clearly better than a server tool, for example HEIC to JPG, OPUS to MP3, MOV to MP4, MP4 to MP3, WebP to JPG, AVIF to JPG and video to GIF.
Launch these as 15 to 25 well-built pages, not hundreds.
Add each following wave only after the previous wave is indexed and showing impressions.

Realistic expectations
A new domain commonly takes months to earn meaningful rankings, and the biggest competitors have years of authority. Success is measured first by pages indexed and impressions, then by clicks, and only later by revenue.
